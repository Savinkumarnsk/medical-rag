# Sample Medical Document — Hypertension Guidelines Summary

## Overview

Hypertension (HTN), also known as high blood pressure, is a chronic medical condition 
in which the blood pressure in the arteries is persistently elevated. Blood pressure 
is expressed by two measurements: systolic (during heart contraction) and diastolic 
(during heart relaxation), measured in millimetres of mercury (mmHg).

## Diagnostic Criteria

According to the 2020 International Society of Hypertension (ISH) Global Hypertension 
Practice Guidelines:

- **Normal BP**: Systolic < 130 mmHg AND Diastolic < 85 mmHg
- **High-normal BP**: Systolic 130–139 mmHg OR Diastolic 85–89 mmHg
- **Grade 1 Hypertension**: Systolic 140–159 mmHg OR Diastolic 90–99 mmHg
- **Grade 2 Hypertension**: Systolic ≥ 160 mmHg OR Diastolic ≥ 100 mmHg

Diagnosis should be confirmed on ≥ 2 occasions, ideally using automated office blood 
pressure (AOBP) or 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM).

## Risk Factors

### Modifiable Risk Factors
- Obesity (BMI > 30 kg/m²)
- Physical inactivity
- High sodium diet (> 5g/day)
- Excessive alcohol consumption (> 14 units/week)
- Smoking and tobacco use
- Dyslipidaemia (elevated LDL cholesterol)
- Diabetes mellitus (DM)
- Chronic kidney disease (CKD)

### Non-Modifiable Risk Factors
- Age (risk increases after age 55 in men, 65 in women)
- Family history of hypertension
- Ethnicity (higher prevalence in African descent populations)

## Complications

Uncontrolled hypertension is a major risk factor for:

1. Myocardial infarction (MI) and heart failure
2. Stroke (ischaemic and haemorrhagic)
3. Chronic kidney disease progression to end-stage renal disease
4. Hypertensive retinopathy and vision loss
5. Peripheral arterial disease (PAD)
6. Aortic aneurysm

## Treatment Guidelines

### Lifestyle Modifications (First-Line for All Grades)

| Intervention | Expected BP Reduction |
|---|---|
| Weight reduction (10 kg) | 5–20 mmHg systolic |
| DASH diet | 8–14 mmHg systolic |
| Sodium restriction (< 2g/day) | 2–8 mmHg systolic |
| Regular aerobic exercise (30 min/day, 5 days/week) | 4–9 mmHg systolic |
| Alcohol reduction | 2–4 mmHg systolic |
| Smoking cessation | Variable (cardiovascular risk reduction) |

### Pharmacological Treatment

#### First-Line Drug Classes
According to the ISH 2020 guidelines, the preferred first-line antihypertensive agents are:

1. **ACE Inhibitors (ACEi)** or **Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs)**
   - Examples: Ramipril 5–10 mg/day; Losartan 50–100 mg/day
   - Preferred in: DM with proteinuria, CKD, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction

2. **Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs)**
   - Examples: Amlodipine 5–10 mg/day; Nifedipine LA 30–60 mg/day
   - Preferred in: Elderly patients, isolated systolic hypertension, African descent patients

3. **Thiazide/Thiazide-like Diuretics**
   - Examples: Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ) 12.5–25 mg/day; Indapamide 1.25–2.5 mg/day
   - Preferred in: Isolated systolic hypertension, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction

#### Combination Therapy
For Grade 2 hypertension or patients not at target after 4 weeks of monotherapy:
- Initiate two-drug combination: preferred ACEi/ARB + CCB or ACEi/ARB + Diuretic
- If target not achieved: three-drug combination (ACEi/ARB + CCB + Diuretic)
- Resistant hypertension (uncontrolled on three drugs): add Spironolactone 25–50 mg/day

#### Treatment Targets
- General population: < 130/80 mmHg (if tolerated)
- Elderly (> 80 years): < 150/90 mmHg (to avoid hypotension)
- Patients with DM: < 130/80 mmHg
- Patients with CKD and proteinuria: < 130/80 mmHg

## Monitoring

- Blood pressure should be rechecked 4 weeks after initiation or dose change
- Electrolytes (K⁺, Na⁺) and renal function (eGFR) should be checked 4 weeks after 
  starting ACEi/ARB or diuretics
- Annual review for end-organ damage: ECG, fundoscopy, urine albumin:creatinine ratio, eGFR

## Hypertensive Emergencies

**Definition**: Severely elevated BP (usually > 180/120 mmHg) WITH evidence of 
acute end-organ damage.

**End-organ damage signs**: Hypertensive encephalopathy, aortic dissection, acute 
pulmonary oedema, acute MI, eclampsia.

**Management**:
- Admit to intensive care unit (ICU)
- Intravenous (IV) antihypertensives: Labetalol, Nicardipine, or Nitroprusside
- Target BP reduction: ≤ 25% within first hour, then gradually to 160/100 over next 6 hours
- Note: Rapid BP reduction can worsen cerebral and renal perfusion

**WARNING**: If you suspect a hypertensive emergency, call emergency services immediately.

## Special Populations

### Pregnancy
- Chronic hypertension in pregnancy: Methyldopa 250–500 mg TDS, or Labetalol 100–200 mg BD
- Pre-eclampsia: Magnesium sulphate for seizure prophylaxis; delivery of foetus is definitive treatment
- ACE inhibitors and ARBs are CONTRAINDICATED in pregnancy (teratogenic)

### Diabetes Mellitus
- ACEi or ARB preferred (renoprotective)
- Target BP: < 130/80 mmHg
- Avoid thiazide diuretics at high doses (worsen glucose control)
